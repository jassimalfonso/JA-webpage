Challenge

[01]

Cut the noise fillers. Keep the person.

Every episode is a long, honest conversation on a couch. Every “um” makes it longer — but cut too much and the hosts stop sounding like themselves. The auto tools I tried cut so hard the tone went flat.

Then the show moved to video, and the job grew: turn each episode into three to six 20–30 second reels, captioned in the show's style, that make someone stop scrolling.

How it works

[03]

  1. Clean by ear

    Ums and uhs go when they break the flow. Fillers that carry rhythm or warmth stay. A blunt rule can't tell “feel like” from “like,”.

  2. Find the 30 seconds

    Each episode gets scanned for moments that stand alone: one idea, a clear start, a line that lands. AI picks are a first draft, not the answer.

  3. Captions that move

    Three words at a time, the spoken word marked, in the show's look — most people watch with the sound off.

  4. Ship on schedule

    Bi-weekly means the edit can't be precious. A repeatable process beats a perfect one-off.

Learned

[04]

The best edit is the one nobody hears.

  • Tone over speed a tighter episode that doesn't sound like them is a worse episode
  • Tools draft, ears decide auto highlights and filler cuts get checked, every time
  • Process beats heroics the hosts have day jobs; the system has to fit around them
