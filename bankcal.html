BankCal

your calories, as a bank account

A daily calorie budget that behaves like a bank account: a balance each morning, every meal a transaction, and a statement that says whether you saved or overspent.

Challenge

[01]

Counting calories is boring banking.

Most trackers just pile up numbers until the end of the day. Nobody knows what 1,840 means in the abstract.

Everyone understands a balance, though. So the budget opens like a bank account each morning, every meal is a withdrawal, a workout is a deposit — and at night you get a statement.

How it works

[03]

  1. Base burn

    Mifflin-St Jeor works out what your body burns at rest, from weight, height, age and sex.

    bmr = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + (male ? 5 : −161)

  2. Daily life, not guesses

    Everyone starts at ×1.2 — a normal day without exercise. Workouts are deposited only when you log them, so activity is never counted twice.

  3. A goal with a floor

    A gentle or steady goal takes 275 or 550 kcal off. The budget never goes below 1,500 (men) or 1,200 (women), whatever the maths says.

    budget = max(floor, round((bmr × 1.2 − goal) / 10) × 10)

  4. The statement

    What you saved turns into something you can feel: 7,700 kcal is about 1 kg of body weight.

Learned

  • Offline first it has to work on a phone with no signal, then sync

  • Tone is a feature a statement that says “well done” gets opened again tomorrow
